<AboutSection id="engineering-focus" title="Engineering Focus">

The problems that interest me sit below the interface: how a service is structured, how its data stays correct, and how it behaves when something goes wrong.

- **Behavior beyond the framework.** Understanding the runtime, database, and network underneath an abstraction, not only its API.
- **Designing backend services.** APIs and service boundaries that stay understandable as a system grows.
- **Data and persistence.** How data is modelled, stored, and kept consistent across relational and NoSQL databases.
- **Asynchronous processing.** Moving work out of the request path with queues, background jobs, and caching, and handling the retries and failures that come with it.
- **Distributed systems and reliability.** How services communicate, fail, and recover, and designing for the failure cases as well as the normal path.
- **Cloud infrastructure and delivery.** How code is built, deployed, and run: CI/CD pipelines, containers, and cloud platforms.

</AboutSection>

<AboutSection id="engineering-approach" title="How I Think About Engineering">

> Show the engineering, don't advertise it.

I would rather show how something works than describe it with adjectives. That principle shapes this portfolio: the work and writing here focus on the engineering itself, which means:

- **Architecture.** How the parts of a system fit together, and why they are separated where they are.
- **Trade-offs.** What a decision costs, and what it was chosen over.
- **Implementation details.** The specifics that make a design work in practice.
- **Reliability.** How a system behaves when a dependency fails, not only when everything works.
- **Data integrity.** Keeping data correct as it moves between people, services, and storage.
- **Real constraints.** Time, scope, and infrastructure limits, stated rather than hidden.

</AboutSection>
